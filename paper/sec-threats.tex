\section{Threats to Validity}\label{sec:threats}
Following Jedlitschka et al.~\cite{jedlitschka2008reporting} and Pinto et al.~\cite{pinto2014energy}, each threat is paired with a mitigation, an ancillary experiment that bounds it, or a statement of its effect on the results. The ancillary experiments are \textbf{AX1}, refresh cadence versus \texttt{powermetrics} interval (done, Section~\ref{sec:meas-energy}); \textbf{AX2}, refresher overhead (preliminary); \textbf{AX3}, IOReport versus \texttt{powermetrics} CPU power (read path); \textbf{AX4}, load scaling with 0 to 10 busy threads (linearity); \textbf{AX5}, calibration of $d$ ($10^4$ sleeps per thread kind and platform; SOCKET round trip); \textbf{AX6}, E-core confinement under BACKGROUND QoS and its frequency effect; and \textbf{AX7}, drift of per-run energy with run index.

\subsection{Construct Validity}
\textbf{Estimated energy.} No wall meter is available. RAPL is validated against physical measurement~\cite{khan2018rapl}; Apple's counters are model estimates without published validation, and \texttt{powermetrics} values are software estimates that Apple advises against comparing across devices~\cite{hubner2025apple}. Hypotheses compare treatments within one machine, so a common bias cancels; AX3 and AX4 check the read path and linearity, not absolute accuracy.
\textbf{Scope and static power.} The counters miss the power supply, SSD, fans, and network, and waiting threads leave the Mac CPU clusters nearly idle (about 0.02\,W), so the cost of waiting longer is mainly static power, seen only in part; hence the idle-subtracted and (Mac) memory-subsystem tests, the SoC report, and a stated limitation.
\textbf{Analytic time floor.} On blocking workloads VT's time advantage over pools with $p < 256$ holds by construction (Section~\ref{sec:planning-variables}), so tests use equal concurrency and smaller pools are reported relative to their floor.
\textbf{Counter staleness} (about 2\% of a window, Section~\ref{sec:meas-energy}) is under half the $\pm 5\%$ equivalence margin, and the profiler fails if the counters stop advancing.
\textbf{Sleep as an I/O proxy.} \texttt{Thread.sleep} involves no device, and its macOS latency depends on system activity (Section~\ref{sec:meas-pitfalls}); AX5 calibrates $d$, and SOCKET adds real loopback I/O.
\textbf{Energy per operation.} Pool creation and teardown are included by definition, which favors SEQ and VT (whose carrier pool persists); JFR thread-start counts bound this, and the SOCKET server's share is measured separately (Section~\ref{sec:planning-units}).
\textbf{Implementations.} PT and recursive FJ differ from the 2014 code, so 2014 comparisons concern shapes and rankings.
\textbf{QoS is not pure placement.} BACKGROUND QoS also lowers frequency and I/O priority and may widen timer coalescing; AX6 and residency logs quantify the former, AX5 calibrates $d$ under both QoS classes, and RQ3.1 on the Mac measures QoS confinement, not core type alone; the asymmetry claim rests on the Intel equal-count arm.

\subsection{Internal Validity}
\textbf{Background load} (Section~\ref{sec:meas-pitfalls}) is controlled by the quiet-machine protocol, the idle gate, and the two gated idle baselines per batch.
\textbf{Run order and power state.} The A1 Intel pilot confounded run order with power state (Section~\ref{sec:meas-pitfalls}). As innocuous setup changes bias measurements~\cite{mytkowicz2009wrong}, we hold the setup fixed and randomize all runs at fork level; Intel CPU-bound runs heat-soak into the sustained regime with per-iteration frequency logging, Mac runs cool down to ``Nominal'' thermal pressure, and AX7 tests for drift.
\textbf{Measurement overhead.} Boundary-only reads avoid polling-induced idle-state suppression~\cite{raffin2025dissecting}, the refresher's overhead (AX2) is constant and part of the idle baseline, and profilers never run during energy runs.
\textbf{JIT and GC.} JMH warmup, a fresh JVM per fork, and a fixed heap stabilize the runtime; construct-induced GC is part of the cost, and JFR attributes it.
\textbf{Uncontrolled DVFS.} Frequencies cannot be fixed on the Mac, and Intel keeps HWP and turbo on, as deployed; frequency is part of the treatment effect and is logged.
\textbf{Core count on Intel.} \texttt{taskset} also changes the processor count the JVM sees, and with it the GC and JIT thread counts; equal-count arms keep these equal across core types.
\textbf{Unexplained FJ behavior.} Mac pilot FJ/SLEEP was faster than FP/SLEEP with equal workers, its CPU energy/op varied strongly (CV 116\%), and one fork ran at FP speed; JFR will examine it on 8~Oct (Table~\ref{tab:schedule}).

\subsection{External Validity}
\textbf{Two machines.} Results come from one Mac mini M4, a mainstream P/E design, and one Intel Core Ultra~7 255H, a three-tier x86 design; cross-platform comparisons concern rankings and curve shapes only (Section~\ref{sec:meas-procedure}). \textbf{Workloads.} Three synthetic kernels with 256 fixed, independent tasks replace the nine 2014 benchmarks; they span CPU-bound, blocking, and network I/O behavior but characterize mechanisms, not application savings. \textbf{Runtime.} One JDK (25, long-term support), one collector, and one OS per platform; results apply to this configuration.

\subsection{Conclusion Validity}
We use non-parametric tests chosen a priori for \textbf{small samples of unknown distribution}, Holm correction within families for \textbf{multiple comparisons}, effect sizes with bootstrap CIs to separate \textbf{significance from magnitude}, and TOST ($\pm 5\%$) before claiming \textbf{null results}. The fork is the unit, which avoids \textbf{pseudo-replication}~\cite{georges2007rigorous}; a split sample by fixed fork index removes \textbf{selection bias in H0-1.2}; a power check on pilot variance confirms the \textbf{sample size}; and \textbf{noise-floor} blocking-workload energy is tested idle-subtracted with a detection limit (Section~\ref{sec:planning-analysis}).

\section{Concluding Remarks}\label{sec:conclusion}
This plan refines the proposal into a two-platform experiment with a sequential baseline, controlled concurrency levels, a socket workload, and an energy profiler piloted on both platforms; Assignment~3 will execute it and publish a replication package.
