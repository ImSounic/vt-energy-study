\subsection{Energy Measurement}\label{sec:meas-energy}
\emph{Instrument.} The primary metric is \texttt{CPU\_Energy}, an IOReport counter with 1\,mJ resolution, verified to be the exact sum of the E and P clusters (ECPU + PCPU), whose split answers RQ3.2; the memory subsystem (DRAM, the AMCC memory controller, and DCS) is tested second on blocking workloads. A C shim called via the Foreign Function \& Memory (FFM) API reads all channels in-process, without root.

\emph{Synchronization and sampling rate.} A custom profiler for the Java Microbenchmark Harness (JMH) reads all counters in its before- and after-iteration hooks and divides each iteration's energy by the operations completed: nothing polls inside the measured region, and the sampling rate is two reads per iteration. On Intel it reads RAPL via powercap, correcting wraparound.

\emph{Refresh.} On macOS~27 the counters advance only at a privileged sampler's ticks (Section~\ref{sec:meas-pitfalls}), so \texttt{powermetrics} runs as root at 100\,ms as a refresher. AX1 showed that without it the counters never advanced, and that at 100\,ms the largest gap between updates was 106\,ms. Both boundary reads can thus be stale by about one refresh interval, but the bias is near zero, because the preceding warmup iteration runs at the same power; we keep about 2\% of a 5\,s window as the bound. A canary check fails loudly if \texttt{CPU\_Energy} stalls for 0.5\,s or more.

\emph{Accuracy and precision.} RAPL has been validated against physical meters~\cite{khan2018rapl}; Apple's counters are unvalidated model estimates (Section~\ref{sec:threats}), and no wall meter is available, so AX3 checks the read path (IOReport against \texttt{powermetrics} CPU power, which share one energy model) and AX4 linearity (load scaling), neither of which validates absolute accuracy.

\subsection{Procedure}\label{sec:meas-procedure}
The 930 Mac\upd{, 280 control, and 318 Intel} runs follow a seeded random order in batches of about 40, each opened by an environment manifest and the refresher start. \emph{Idle baselines:} each batch takes a 60\,s idle baseline at its start and at its end, each preceded by the quiet gate; the two must agree within 10\% \upd{(on the Mac within $\max(10\%, 10\,\mathrm{mW})$, because the two quiet pilot baselines differed by 32\% at about 0.02\,W)}, or the batch is \upd{re-run} (pitfall~(5), Section~\ref{sec:meas-pitfalls}). \emph{Per run:} (1)~the gate requires $\geq$95\% CPU idle over 3\,s; (2)~one JMH invocation runs one fork of a cell, one trial (Section~\ref{sec:planning-design}), with \mbox{\texttt{-f 1}} \mbox{\texttt{-wi 5}} \mbox{\texttt{-w 1s}} \mbox{\texttt{-i 5}} \mbox{\texttt{-r 5s}} \mbox{\texttt{-rf json}}: five 1\,s warmup iterations, after which the Mac pilot's measured iterations were flat (within-fork CV below 2\% in CPU-bound cells, so the Mac needs no heat-soak), and five 5\,s measured iterations; VT's carrier count is set per JVM (Table~\ref{tab:variables}), and BACKGROUND runs pass a \mbox{\texttt{taskpolicy -b}} wrapper to JMH's \mbox{\texttt{-jvm}} option; (3)~a cooldown of at least 10\,s lasts, on the Mac, until thermal pressure is ``Nominal''.

\emph{Power regime.} On Intel, CPU-bound runs warm up for at least 60\,s (a heat-soak), beyond the boost phase of about 11\,s in the A1 Intel pilot, so the measured window lies in the sustained regime, the intended one because it corresponds to long-running services; the core frequency is logged per iteration from cpufreq sysfs, so the measured regime is explicit, and iterations crossing a regime change are flagged. The 2~Oct Intel pilot confirmed one regime per fork (Section~\ref{sec:meas-pitfalls}). Blocking workloads draw too little power to reach any limit. The Intel package domain idles at 3.65\,W and the Mac CPU clusters at 0.020\,W, so the instruments count different things at zero load, and cross-platform comparisons are of rankings and curve shapes only.

\emph{Protocol and data.} The quiet-machine protocol quits all applications, enables Do Not Disturb, disables Wi-Fi, pauses Spotlight indexing, sleeps the display, and holds the system awake with \texttt{caffeinate}. \upd{The gate also rejects a list of applications (browsers, Electron apps), each idle baseline must lie below a ceiling (0.08\,W Mac, 6\,W Intel), and the Intel runner refuses to start unless every core's EPP is \texttt{balance\_performance}. The Mac stayed on Wi-Fi, because it was operated over SSH.} Each run yields a JMH JSON file; a schedule CSV, the \texttt{powermetrics} or cpufreq and \texttt{coretemp} logs, and the idle baselines complete the data.

\subsection{Profiling}\label{sec:meas-profiling}
Java Flight Recorder (JFR) attributes Java-level causes (thread starts, parking, GC) in separate passes, never during energy runs; on the Mac pilot's SLEEP workload at $p=8$, VT against PT showed 19 vs.\ 189,191 OS thread starts and 1 vs.\ 54 GCs. \upd{The Mac profile campaign ran; Intel profiling with \texttt{perf stat} did not (Section~\ref{sec:planning-design}).}

\subsection{Familiarity with the Infrastructure and Pitfalls}\label{sec:meas-pitfalls}
Three pilots exercised the pipeline end to end: the A1 Intel pilot (RAPL, JDK~26.0.2, $p=8$, 2 forks $\times$ 10 $\times$ 1\,s), the Mac feasibility pilot of 1~Oct, and the 2~Oct Intel pilot (Temurin 25.0.4.1, 60\,s heat-soak), \upd{each at $p = 8$ with 3 forks (pilot data in the replication package)}. They exposed five pitfalls.

\emph{(1) Power-regime shift (Intel).} In the A1 Intel pilot, PT on CPU drew 62\,J per 1\,s iteration in fork~1 up to iteration~5 and 25\,J from iteration~8, while time/op rose from 89 to 128\,ms; fork~2 drew 25\,J throughout. This is a power-regime shift \upd{to the firmware's sustained limit: the binding PL1 is 25\,W in the MMIO RAPL interface, not the 65\,W of the MSR that A2 reported as not reached}. JMH's fixed cell order confounded run order with power state. \emph{Response:} randomized fork order, the heat-soak with frequency logging and flagging (Section~\ref{sec:meas-procedure}), and AX7; in the 2~Oct pilot every CPU-bound fork stayed in one regime (within-fork CV 0.3 to 3.6\%).

\emph{(2) Frozen and stale counters (macOS~27).} Counters update only at sampler ticks (AX1): a 1\,s burn read without proper refresh gave 1100\,mJ, which equals the \emph{prior} sample (1077\,mW $\times$ 1.01\,s); the burn itself drew 8149\,mW. \emph{Response:} 100\,ms refresh, 5\,s windows, canary check.

\emph{(3) Sleep overshoot (macOS).} FP/SLEEP at $p=8$ has a nominal floor of 320\,ms, yet took 458.6\,ms on the quiet Mac and 327.9\,ms on Linux: macOS sleep latency depends on system activity, plausibly through timer coalescing. \emph{Response:} AX5 calibrates $d$ per platform, and SOCKET adds real I/O.

\emph{(4) Background load.} Idle CPU power was 0.020\,W on the quiet Mac against 0.184\,W with everyday applications open (about 9$\times$). \emph{Response:} the quiet-machine protocol and gate.

\emph{(5) Contaminated idle baseline (Intel).} The 2~Oct pilot's end-of-run baseline was taken after the operator had returned: package power read 14.99\,W against 3.65\,W at the start. \emph{Response:} the quiet gate precedes every idle baseline, and the two baselines of a batch must agree (Section~\ref{sec:meas-procedure}).

\emph{Pilot observations.} \upd{On Intel under SLEEP, package power for FP, FJ, and VT equalled the idle baseline within 0.2\,W, whereas PT drew about 6.5\,W: creating 256 OS threads per operation is measurable even while waiting.}

\upd{\emph{Pitfalls during execution.} (6)~\emph{Applications open (Intel).} A 24~Oct Intel pilot with a browser and the Claude desktop app open, under the \texttt{performance} profile, idled at 8.5 to 14\,W instead of 3.65\,W, and no batch's idle pair agreed; it was discarded, and the application list, idle ceiling, and EPP check above were added. (7)~\emph{OS update (Mac).} An automatic update (27.2 beta to 27.2) rebooted the Mac during the first Mac campaign; those runs were set aside, the campaign was rerun in full on one build, and the runner now stops if the build changes. (8)~\emph{Socket backlog (Mac).} With macOS's default listen backlog (\texttt{kern.ipc.somaxconn} = 128), 256 simultaneous connections were reset; it is raised to 1024 after each boot. (9)~\emph{Pool lifetime.} Per-operation pool creation dominated FP and FJ on blocking workloads; the control experiment (Section~\ref{sec:planning-design}) measures it.}
