\subsection{Energy Measurement}\label{sec:meas-energy}
\emph{Instrument.} The primary metric is \texttt{CPU\_Energy}, an IOReport counter with 1\,mJ resolution, verified to be the exact sum of the E and P clusters (ECPU + PCPU), whose split answers RQ3.2; the memory subsystem (DRAM, the AMCC memory controller, and DCS) is tested second on blocking workloads. A C shim called via the Foreign Function \& Memory (FFM) API reads all channels in-process, without root.

\emph{Synchronization and sampling rate.} A custom profiler for the Java Microbenchmark Harness (JMH) reads all counters in its before- and after-iteration hooks and divides each iteration's energy by the operations completed: nothing polls inside the measured region, and the sampling rate is two reads per iteration. On Intel it reads RAPL via powercap, correcting wraparound.

\emph{Refresh.} On macOS~27 the counters advance only at a privileged sampler's ticks (Section~\ref{sec:meas-pitfalls}), so \texttt{powermetrics} runs as root at 100\,ms as a refresher. AX1 (done) measured the refresh over 6\,s idle windows: with \texttt{powermetrics} off the counters never advanced, and at 1000, 250, 100, and 50\,ms intervals they advanced 13, 47, 111, and 208 times, with maximum gaps of 1005.6, 256.8, 106.0, and 55.9\,ms. Both boundary reads can thus be stale by up to about one refresh interval (106\,ms), but the bias is near zero, because the warmup iteration preceding the first measured one runs at the same power, so energy missed at one boundary is matched at the other; we keep about 2\% of a 5\,s window as the bound. A canary check fails loudly if \texttt{CPU\_Energy} stalls for 0.5\,s or more.

\emph{Accuracy and precision.} RAPL has been validated against physical meters~\cite{khan2018rapl}; Apple's counters are unvalidated model estimates (Section~\ref{sec:threats}), and no wall meter is available, so AX3 checks the read path (IOReport against \texttt{powermetrics} CPU power, which share one energy model) and AX4 linearity (load scaling), neither of which validates absolute accuracy.

The 930 Mac and 705 Intel runs follow a seeded random order in batches of about 40, each opened by an environment manifest and the refresher start. \emph{Idle baselines:} each batch takes a 60\,s idle baseline at its start and at its end, each preceded by the quiet gate; the two must agree within 10\%, or the batch is discarded (pitfall~(5), Section~\ref{sec:meas-pitfalls}). \emph{Per run:} (1)~the gate requires $\geq$95\% CPU idle over 3\,s; (2)~one JMH invocation runs one fork of a cell, one trial (Section~\ref{sec:planning-design}), with \mbox{\texttt{-f 1}} \mbox{\texttt{-wi 5}} \mbox{\texttt{-w 1s}} \mbox{\texttt{-i 5}} \mbox{\texttt{-r 5s}} \mbox{\texttt{-rf json}}: five 1\,s warmup iterations, after which the Mac pilot's measured iterations were flat (within-fork CV below 2\%, so the Mac needs no heat-soak), and five 5\,s measured iterations; VT's carrier count is set per JVM (Table~\ref{tab:variables}), and BACKGROUND runs pass a \mbox{\texttt{taskpolicy -b}} wrapper to JMH's \mbox{\texttt{-jvm}} option; (3)~a cooldown of at least 10\,s lasts, on the Mac, until thermal pressure is ``Nominal''.

\emph{Power regime.} On Intel, CPU-bound runs warm up for at least 60\,s (a heat-soak), beyond the boost phase of about 11\,s in the A1 Intel pilot, so the measured window lies in the sustained regime, the intended one because it corresponds to long-running services; the core frequency is logged per iteration from cpufreq sysfs, so the measured regime is explicit, and iterations crossing a regime change are flagged. The 2~Oct Intel pilot confirmed one regime per fork (Section~\ref{sec:meas-pitfalls}). Blocking workloads draw too little power to reach any limit. The Intel package domain idles at 3.65\,W and the Mac CPU clusters at 0.020\,W, so the instruments count different things at zero load, and cross-platform comparisons are of rankings and curve shapes only.

\emph{Protocol and data.} The quiet-machine protocol quits all applications, enables Do Not Disturb, disables Wi-Fi, pauses Spotlight indexing, sleeps the display, and holds the system awake with \texttt{caffeinate}. Each run yields a JMH JSON file; a schedule CSV, the \texttt{powermetrics} or cpufreq and \texttt{coretemp} logs, and the idle baselines complete the data.

\subsection{Profiling}\label{sec:meas-profiling}
Java Flight Recorder (JFR, \texttt{profile} settings) attributes Java-level causes (thread starts, park and sleep, GC, hot methods); \texttt{powermetrics} records wakeups and cluster residency; on Intel, \texttt{perf stat} counts context switches, migrations, and cycles per core type. Each runs in separate passes (2 forks per selected cell), never during energy runs. JFR on the Mac pilot's SLEEP workload at $p=8$ already exposes the mechanism: VT against PT showed 19 vs.\ 189,191 OS thread starts, 1 vs.\ 54 GCs, and 686 vs.\ 2,252 allocation samples.

\subsection{Familiarity with the Infrastructure and Pitfalls}\label{sec:meas-pitfalls}
Three pilots exercised the pipeline end to end: the A1 Intel pilot (RAPL, JDK~26.0.2, $p=8$, 2 forks $\times$ 10 $\times$ 1\,s), the Mac feasibility pilot of 1~Oct, and the 2~Oct Intel pilot (Temurin 25.0.4.1, 60\,s heat-soak), the latter two as in Table~\ref{tab:pilot}. They exposed five pitfalls.

\emph{(1) Power-regime shift (Intel).} In the A1 Intel pilot, under JDK~26.0.2 and an identical treatment, PT on CPU drew 62\,J per 1\,s iteration in fork~1 until iteration~6 and 25\,J from iteration~8, while time/op rose from 89 to 128\,ms; fork~2 drew 25\,J throughout. This is a power-regime shift of unconfirmed cause, plausibly platform thermal or embedded-controller policy; the RAPL limits (PL1 65\,W, PL2 115\,W) were not reached. JMH's fixed cell order confounded run order with power state, so our A1 CPU-bound figures (3.38 vs.\ 5.13\,J/op) averaged over both regimes. \emph{Response:} randomized fork order, the heat-soak with frequency logging and flagging (Section~\ref{sec:meas-procedure}), and AX7. In the 2~Oct Intel pilot the heat-soak held every CPU-bound fork in one regime: within-fork CV of package energy per iteration 0.3 to 3.6\%, fork-to-fork CV of J/op 0.1 to 0.6\%, \texttt{coretemp} 56 to 60\,$^\circ$C, and no recurrence of the 62 to 25\,J drop; on the M4, thermal pressure stayed ``Nominal'' in all 8094 pilot samples.

\emph{(2) Frozen and stale counters (macOS~27).} Counters update only at sampler ticks (AX1): a 1\,s burn read without proper refresh gave 1100\,mJ, the \emph{prior} sample (1077\,mW $\times$ 1.01\,s), not the burn (8149\,mW). \emph{Response:} 100\,ms refresh, 5\,s windows, canary check.

\emph{(3) Sleep overshoot (macOS).} The analytic floor (Section~\ref{sec:planning-variables}) of FP/SLEEP at $p=8$ is nominally 320\,ms, yet the quiet Mac took 458.6\,ms and a busy machine the same day 387\,ms: sleep latency depends on system activity, plausibly through timer coalescing. On Linux, FP took 327.9\,ms, an overshoot of 2.5\% against 43\% on macOS, so the pitfall is macOS-specific. \emph{Response:} AX5 calibrates $d$ per platform, measured latency is reported, and SOCKET adds real I/O.

\emph{(4) Background load.} Idle CPU power was 0.020\,W on the quiet Mac against 0.184\,W with everyday applications open (about 9$\times$). \emph{Response:} the quiet-machine protocol and gate.

\emph{(5) Contaminated idle baseline (Intel).} The end-of-run baseline of the 2~Oct pilot was taken after the operator had returned to the machine: package power read 14.99\,W against 3.65\,W at the start, and uncore 7.10\,W against 0.05\,W. \emph{Response:} the quiet gate precedes every idle baseline, and a batch's two baselines must agree within 10\% (Section~\ref{sec:meas-procedure}).

\emph{Pilot observations.} Table~\ref{tab:pilot} supports no conclusion about which construct is greener (single $p$, three forks, flat FJ). On the Mac under SLEEP, CPU energy is at the noise floor (0.09 to 0.7\,J per 5\,s window for FP, FJ and VT; idle-subtracted J/op within idle noise), unlike memory-subsystem energy (Section~\ref{sec:planning-design}). The refresher's overhead is at most 13\,mW (preliminary AX2), comparable to sleep-bound CPU power but constant across treatments; because the refresher is itself a 10\,Hz poller, AX2 compares idle power at 100, 250, and 1000\,ms intervals to trade staleness against wakeups. On Intel under SLEEP, package power for FP, FJ, and VT equals the idle baseline within 0.1\,W, so energy/op equals idle power times time/op and the idle-subtracted value is zero within noise, whereas PT draws about 6.5\,W (core domain 13.2\,J against 2.8\,J per window for VT): creating 256 OS threads per operation is measurable even while waiting.
